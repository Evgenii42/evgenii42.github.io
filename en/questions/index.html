Experience and motivation

How would you introduce yourself professionally?+

I am a product manager with an engineering and project background. I have worked with industrial systems, international clients, retail and CRM, and I develop my own ecosystem, EmTalk. I am drawn to complex problems and bringing people together around a working solution.

What kinds of product problems interest you?+

Creating new products, tackling complex problems and working with uncertainty. My focus spans more than one product type: B2B and B2C, research, teams, business outcomes and new tools. EmTalk and the AI direction show how those interests shape my work.

Industrial automation, oil and gas, digital twins, retail, sales CRM and employee monitoring. At EmTalk, our directions cover well-being, self-understanding, decision-making and Tapl education. Product stages differ: I do not describe Tapl as a released product.

Decisions and outcomes

Do you have experience beyond delivery?+

Yes: discovery, product strategy, analytics and developing my own products. According to my account, Kickidler LTV grew by 20%. At Samolet, I work with conversion and initiatives with a forecast effect of approximately RUB 500 million. That forecast is not realised revenue.

How do you handle conflicting interests?+

On WinLab assortment planning, I expanded the stakeholder group and gathered other perspectives when direct discussion stopped moving the work forward. This helped us progress to business and functional requirements. The task is to bring the conversation back to the problem and the conditions for solving it.

Describe a decision you had to reconsider.+

At EmTalk, we moved away from the original super-app and split the value into several directions. Later, we paused narrative stories and deep gamification because available resources and skills did not support the quality we needed. An appealing idea can still be unsuitable for the current stage.

How do you work with uncertainty?+

I separate what is known from what needs investigation. At Statcontrol, I proposed separating R&D from more predictable engineering execution. At EmTalk, we explored the Tapl concept through qualitative tests and customer interviews with external users. Positive feedback is not proof of product-market fit.

Methods and teams

Why use Current and Future Reality Trees?+

The first connects symptoms to possible causes; the second examines the logic of proposed changes. I used them in Statcontrol and Kickidler analysis. A tree structures a hypothesis about a system, but does not prove every causal link.

Statcontrol: when decisions depend on indispensable leaders →Kickidler: finding the causes of delay instead of asking for more speed →
How do you develop a team?+

Through clear roles, joint work and support for skills. At Kickidler, I mentored analysts and worked on documentation. At EmTalk, I adapted Spotify principles for a startup; some contributors have stayed for two years or longer.

Kickidler: supporting the people expected to change →The EmTalk team: what helps people keep working together →
What are you still improving in your approach?+

At Samolet, I received feedback that I did not always have enough time for manual checks before a demo. I aim to address this systematically through earlier QA involvement, clear requirements and checks. This is an area for improvement, not a claim that personal verification is unnecessary.

How do you connect change to economics?+

For the Statcontrol transformation proposal, I prepared a model of costs, cash flows, NPV and risks. It helped frame the proposal as an investment decision. A calculated scenario must remain distinct from a realised outcome.

AI in practice

How do you use AI in your work?+

For research, preparing materials, prototyping and automation. At Samolet, I built a Python and Streamlit analytics tool with AI assistance. At EmTalk, we develop product and content tools using LLMs, while this portfolio is developed with agents and specifications.

No product analyst yet: building the tool myself →EmTalk: from sources to an article and a narration script →Working with AI: context, specifications and a verifiable result →
What does RAG contribute, and where do you use it?+

The EmTalk content workflow retrieves material from a knowledge base before generation. Full-text and semantic search complement each other, while metadata preserves source provenance. RAG organises context but does not guarantee a correct answer. For Decision Matrix, RAG remains a development direction.

EmTalk: build the knowledge base before asking AI to write →Decision Matrix: AI suggests options, people make the decision →
How do you evaluate AI output?+

I distinguish format, content and usefulness. Code can validate response structure, another model can assist editing, and a person remains responsible for assessing the material. The inspected tools have different checkpoints, but a review status does not prove that a person has already checked everything.

EmTalk: from sources to an article and a narration script →EmTalk: a course is built in stages, not with one AI prompt →Scripts and music: creative choices within verifiable constraints →
How do SDD, OpenSpec and skills help?+

Specifications preserve requirements and acceptance scenarios; skills and instructions describe an agent workflow. This project keeps facts, changes, verification results and Git versions separately. That helps retain context and verify a specific result.
