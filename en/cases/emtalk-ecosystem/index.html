Product decision

A shift from the super-app idea to four connected directions, with work continuing through a new team.

The strategy change, team transition and unit-economics work are based on my account. Four directions do not mean four released apps; this case does not establish profitability or product-market fit.

Reconsider the scope

After joining Astana Hub, we moved into intensive development. Some contributors were growing tired and no longer saw a reason to continue. The original super-app idea and our chosen technical approach were a poor fit for our capacity. We decided to change both how we worked and the product concept itself.

Separate the user needs

We split the value into four needs: finding calm, understanding your current state, making a decision and taking action. The last direction initially resembled a to-do tool and later became the starting point for rethinking Tapl. Separate products would share a common logic without depending on the completion of one large app.

Continue with a new team

We brought together a new development team and continued. Alongside development, we explored how to make the products recognisable, worked on unit economics and considered our route to market. I kept returning to three questions: what we are building, how to build it and how to sell it. At that point, our understanding of development was stronger than our answers to the other two questions.

One idea, different stages

BRET and Decision Matrix reached Google Play, while the self-understanding direction works through tests and articles. Tapl followed a different path: we had to pause part of its more complex game-based concept. The ecosystem describes the connection between directions, not equal readiness across all products.
