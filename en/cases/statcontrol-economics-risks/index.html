Statcontrol: testing the economics of change before implementation

An organisational idea also needs a case for cost, payback and risk. How I turned a proposal into a financial model.

  • AnalyticsAnalytics
  • Product strategyProduct strategy
Business case

Prepared a model of costs, cash flows, NPV and implementation risks.

My analytical work from 2022. NPV, payback and simulation outputs are forecasts; no independent recalculation is claimed.

How I approached it

  1. Work and resource costs
  2. Cash-flow assumptions
  3. NPV and payback
  4. Stakeholders and risks
  5. Decision rationale

An organisation chart was not enough

The OptiRamp transformation proposal needed an estimate of its cost and potential payback. There was also a need to make individual project economics more transparent: a single expert estimate does not reveal how costs develop during delivery.

Breaking down the cost

I proposed separating labour, equipment, subcontractors and preparation costs. The model considered the time between estimation and the start of work, as well as spending during execution. This made assumptions visible instead of reducing the discussion to one total.

Modelling the expected effect

I prepared projected cash flows, NPV and discounted payback to assess the changes. Inputs included approximations and simplifications. The model was a tool for evaluating a scenario, not a statement of realised savings or revenue.

Examining what could go wrong

I also prepared a stakeholder register and risk analysis. I used probability and impact assessment and Monte Carlo schedule modelling in Primavera Risk Analysis. These were forecasts based on expert assumptions, not guaranteed completion dates.

Connecting change to the business

The organisational proposal gained a financial and risk assessment, allowing it to be discussed as an investment decision. This case does not claim a realised financial outcome from that model.
