EmTalk / Founder / Product Owner

The first team broke apart: continuing EmTalk

We started with a B2C product idea and a team that wanted to keep working after the incubator. Our first major turning point came in the relationships between team members.

After the original team broke apart, most of its members continued working on EmTalk with me.

My account, recorded in October 2026. The conflict is described from my perspective. The project story is still unfolding.

The team wanted to keep going

After I left the startup incubator, members of the team approached me because they did not want to stop. I decided to continue too. In 2023, we began developing an idea for a B2C mental health product. My wife suggested using the user journey of a period and pregnancy tracking app as a reference. That became the starting point for the first EmTalk concept.

Bring people together around the idea

I worked on strategy and took on the lead founder role. We divided responsibilities, including marketing and design, began refining the value proposition and looked for contributors. We approached graduates of GeekBrains, Netology and other schools. The team researched the audience and competitors. We adapted approaches we knew from the incubator to fit our own project.

When trust broke down

Later, I noticed that some information was not reaching the whole team. From my perspective, one participant was keeping information to themselves rather than sharing it fully. I said that I could no longer trust them. The original team broke apart. We agreed that everything developed up to that point would be available to every participant, and that each person could continue independently.

The decision to continue

I wanted to keep developing the project. Most of the team stayed with me, even though I had not worked closely with everyone before. EmTalk continued with a changed team. This was the first major turning point in our team story; decisions about our development model and learning came at a later stage.
