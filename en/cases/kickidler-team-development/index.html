Kickidler / Product Manager · team development

Kickidler: supporting the people expected to change

A new process needs more than rules. How I worked on analysis practices, team development and feedback.

  • StakeholdersStakeholders
Mentoring and development plan

Started supporting analysts and included learning and communication in the change programme.

My Kickidler team document and subsequent clarification: team eNPS, not customer NPS. Detailed survey measurements have not been provided.

How I approached it

  1. Working-context diagnosis
  2. Template and documentation
  3. Analyst mentoring
  4. Development and feedback plan
  5. Team observations

Change depended on everyday work

The Kickidler diagnosis showed that delivery and quality problems were accompanied by difficulties with documentation, information exchange and motivation. More systematic work required practical support and usable tools.

Starting with analysis practices

I prepared an analyst template, started documentation work and provided mentoring. The task was to develop analysis practices with the people writing requirements, rather than only describe a desired process.

Including development in the plan

The change programme included learning, cross-functional work, mentoring and knowledge management. I proposed individual development plans and regular feedback. These were parts of the proposal; not every element is documented as implemented.

Looking beyond delivery dates

I identified team satisfaction, requirements quality and stakeholder feedback as areas to assess. I later reported improved motivation and team eNPS. Survey values, sample and dates have not been supplied, so this remains a qualitative account.

What this experience demonstrates

This episode connects analysis, processes and team development. Analyst support had begun and the change plan was prepared; the improvement in team sentiment is based on my account. The overall 20% LTV increase remains in the Kickidler overview and is not attributed to mentoring.
