EmTalk: a course is built in stages, not with one AI prompt

A long piece of text does not automatically become a course. EmTalk's educational workflow separates structure, lesson planning, materials and knowledge checks into distinct stages.

Related processes for preparing course structure, plans, lessons and assessments, with editable documents and review statuses.

Based on tool configuration inspected on 10 October 2026. This describes the team workflow; execution outcomes and resource savings were not measured in this review.

  6. Documents and review statuses

Separate decisions that should not happen all at once

In EmTalk's AI direction, course preparation is divided into several processes. Objectives, structure and milestones come first, followed by a lesson plan, learning materials and assessments. This makes intermediate decisions visible: the structure can be worked on before a large amount of text exists.

Connect structure to the plan

The first process uses RAG context and produces an editable course structure in a spreadsheet. Code checks required fields and that milestone durations add up to the total period. The Planner reads this structure, assigns lessons to known milestones and adds intermediate and final assessments. Lessons receive an order and links to subsequent stages.

Base assessment on the material prepared for learning

An ordinary lesson retrieves context from the knowledge base. An assessment takes a different route: it collects the text of the already generated lessons listed in its plan. This lets the learning materials define the assessment's scope, instead of asking the model for an arbitrary quiz on a broad topic.

Validate structure and leave room for instructional review

The parser requires knowledge-check and answer sections, a specified number of questions and A/B/C options. These rules catch some structural problems before handoff. They do not establish the correctness of every answer or the effectiveness of learning. Materials remain editable, while structure, plan, lesson and assessment statuses provide separate review points.

Choose between staged work and batch generation

The Writer supports commands for the next material, a specific lesson or the full sequence. Batch mode reduces the number of manual commands, but does not by itself mean that a person approved each document. I therefore describe this as preparation of learning materials with opportunities for review, not an autonomous teacher or a validated educational method.
