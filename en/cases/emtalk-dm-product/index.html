EmTalk / Founder / Product Manager

Decision Matrix: AI suggests options, people make the decision

Choosing a project, a career move or one of several ideas can be difficult. At EmTalk, we are developing a product that helps people see their options, compare them and understand the reasoning behind a choice.

Published app

Decision Matrix is available on Google Play. Its published release describes AI-generated alternatives, option comparison and decision history.

Android features are supported by the official Google Play listing and DM page checked on 10 October 2026; the app was not run during this review. The founder role is based on my account. RAG and team scenarios belong to later releases in BRD 2.0. No measured impact, product-market fit or commercial success is claimed here.

When advice alone is not enough

The question 'What should I choose?' often hides competing priorities. Someone considering a job may value both growth and stability. A ready-made answer does not explain which trade-offs they are willing to make. Decision Matrix addresses this problem by making the structure of a choice visible.

Turn options into a comparison

A person frames the question, adds options and compares them. A matrix with criteria and weights helps show how priorities affect the result. They can save a decision and return to it later. This gives the choice a visible rationale that they can revisit.

Where AI helps

Sometimes the first difficulty is knowing which options to consider. AI can suggest alternatives, and users can add their own. The product page also describes AI support for exploring pros and cons. A model's suggestion becomes input for comparison; the final choice remains with the user.

My responsibility and the team's work

As EmTalk's founder, I am responsible for developing the products and attracting their first users. We develop Decision Matrix as a team. The app reached Google Play with AI-generated alternatives, comparison and decision history. That gives us a concrete product from which to continue testing its value.

Beyond the released version

Our business requirements describe a longer journey: revisit decisions, refine criteria and apply them to new questions. A knowledge base for RAG and team decision-making are part of the proposed development. These are design directions, rather than a list of released capabilities. The current product and its possible evolution remain distinct.
