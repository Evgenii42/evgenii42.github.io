The team archive, dated to early 2024 by me, and my account of my role. The workbook does not establish achieved product-market fit or B2B sales.

From a broad idea to user needs

We prepared the workbook in early 2024. It explored stress, burnout, workplace relationships, motivation and decision-making. I worked on strategy and the value proposition, while the team conducted research and competitor analysis. In the canvases and audience segmentation, we described who might benefit from the product and in which situations. The working profiles helped us frame user needs, but remained audience models.

Explore B2C and B2B

We considered two models. For B2C, we explored ways to support individuals. For B2B, we discussed the needs of HR teams and companies, adapted content and client analytics. We gathered recruitment channels, outreach messages and interview guides, and compared competitors. Working comments show discussion of segments and questions: where to narrow a prompt, ask about past experience or investigate how someone already handles a problem.

Separate the concept into stages

The Product matrix divides features into prototype, MVP, target vision and future plans. This format let us discuss the initial scope separately from more complex possibilities. AI-based problem diagnosis and help with solving problems, for example, were placed in later stages. This was a map of proposed development, not a list of features already released.

What the research archive contains

At this stage, we had prepared a foundation for further product discovery: model options, research tools and feature boundaries. The workbook combines hypotheses, some records of findings and plans. It does not provide a complete chain from interview to tested change for every decision. These materials are outdated and describe our search in early 2024; the current EmTalk apps and directions are covered in a separate overview.
