EmTalk: build the knowledge base before asking AI to write

A well-written answer does not necessarily rest on suitable sources. In EmTalk's AI workflow, preparing knowledge is a separate process: find material, preserve its provenance and assemble context for the task.

  • AI & automationAI & automation
Configured knowledge workflow

Enrichment from five scientific services, a separate path for manually selected sources and hybrid retrieval for content preparation.

Based on tool configuration inspected on 10 October 2026. This describes the team workflow; execution outcomes and resource savings were not measured in this review.

From a source to useful context

  1. Topic and search queries
  2. Collect and normalise material
  3. Deduplicate and preserve provenance
  4. Index and verify storage
  5. Retrieve by wording and meaning
  6. Assemble task-specific context

Treat knowledge as a separate product component

At EmTalk, I develop the AI direction as part of our product work. One workflow prepares the knowledge base for articles and courses. The task begins before text generation: collect material, distinguish source types and retain the origin of a claim. The configuration separates knowledge enrichment from subsequent generation.

Combine sources without erasing their differences

Search connects to OpenAlex, Europe PMC, Semantic Scholar, Crossref and ClinicalTrials.gov. Material is normalised and deduplicated by DOI, registry identifier or title. Provenance, dates, material type and other metadata are retained alongside the text. Clinical trial registry records remain separate from scientific publications: a registry entry alone is not evidence of efficacy.

Search by wording and meaning

Material is split into chunks and indexed in PostgreSQL with PGVector. Full-text search finds matches in wording, while semantic search retrieves related meaning. Reciprocal Rank Fusion combines the results, removes duplicates and adds a metadata-based source weighting. Context therefore does not depend on a single retrieval method.

Make room for manual selection

Automated discovery is complemented by a separate route for links selected by a person: extract text, check for duplicates, index and verify storage. This provides a way to add material an operator considers important. Selecting a source and using it in a particular generation workflow remain distinct concerns.

Check readiness without claiming certainty

The workflow checks that chunks were stored and that automated enrichment includes several distinct scientific sources. Metadata helps rank results, but a heuristic score does not replace expert appraisal of a publication. Some source material consists of abstracts. I therefore describe this stage as preparing context for further work, not automatically establishing truth.
