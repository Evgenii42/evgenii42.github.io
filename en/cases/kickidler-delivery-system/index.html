Kickidler: finding the causes of delay instead of asking for more speed

How I connected technical constraints, requirements and communication in one diagnosis, then developed a change plan.

Diagnosis and initial changes

Designed a target model and started introducing planning and requirements practices.

Based on my Kickidler document and reported team outcomes. Time-to-market boundaries and comparison periods have not been published.

How I approached it

  1. Observed problems
  2. Current Reality Tree
  3. Planning and requirements
  4. Future Reality Tree
  5. Phased change plan

Several problems reinforced each other

At Kickidler, delivery dates, quality and team collaboration needed attention at the same time. My diagnosis linked technical constraints to testing difficulties, while unclear requirements and communication added rework. A list of complaints alone did not explain where to start.

Building a Current Reality Tree

I mapped relationships between untracked work, defects, development speed, initiative and motivation. The Current Reality Tree helped form hypotheses about causes and dependencies. These were working hypotheses to test through changes and observation.

Starting with work visibility

I began introducing planning and a Gantt-based roadmap. I prepared an analyst template and started documentation work. These were concrete first steps towards clearer tasks and expectations, rather than evidence that the entire target process had been implemented.

Designing the target state

In the Future Reality Tree and change plan, I connected engineering tools, testing, documentation and cross-functional work. The plan covered preparation, implementation, monitoring and adaptation, with explicit attention to communication and learning.

What changed and what can be concluded

According to my account, the team halved time-to-market, reduced bugs and improved analytics. These were overall team outcomes. I do not attribute them to one diagram, template or technical change; the document alone does not establish completion of every planned action.
