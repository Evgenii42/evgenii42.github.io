EmTalk / Founder / Product Owner

Tapl: rethink learning and decide what to pause

Tapl grew from a to-do concept into an EdTech direction built around routines, sprints and metrics. External users gave positive feedback, but the more complex game-based work needed expertise we could not consistently access.

Research and a scope decision

Positive feedback from external users in qualitative tests and customer discovery. We paused narrative stories and deeper gamification after roughly a year and a half of work.

Based on my account and clarification that research involved external users. Sample size and quantitative test results are not specified; positive feedback does not establish product-market fit. This story concerns the paused game-based work and does not claim a public Tapl release or a scheduled restart.

More than a task list

The original Tapl concept resembled a task list. After discussion, we decided that this offered too little value. We developed an EdTech concept: courses adapt to the user's routine, learning follows sprints and tracks progress metrics. We wanted to explore how learning could fit into someone's life, beyond simply delivering content.

Explore the idea beyond the team

We ran qualitative tests and customer discovery with external users. We received positive feedback on the idea.

Match complexity to capacity

Narrative stories and deeper gamification were intended to support learning. Access to narrative designers became a constraint: experienced specialists could contribute as mentors, while the less experienced team lacked the capacity to deliver the intended quality. We could not resolve that gap within our unpaid working model.

Leave room to return

After roughly a year and a half of work, we paused the narrative stories and deeper gamification. I would consider returning to that part of Tapl if the right resources and expertise become available. For now, that is an intention; the pause was a way to align the idea with what the team could deliver.
